Government set-aside contracts can give qualified small businesses a fairer path into federal procurement—but only if you can find the right opportunities before the deadline. The good news is that SAM.gov has powerful search tools. The challenge is turning thousands of notices into a focused pipeline.

## Start with a precise SAM.gov search

Create a saved search on SAM.gov and begin with the **Contract Opportunities** domain. Use your relevant NAICS codes, place of performance, and a realistic dollar range. Search by both NAICS and keywords: agencies do not always describe the same work with the same language.

Review the notice type and response deadline before investing time in a document. A Sources Sought notice is market research, not a solicitation; a Presolicitation signals planning; and an active Solicitation is the opportunity requiring a proposal now.

## Use set-aside filters deliberately

Under “Set Aside,” select the certifications your company actually holds—such as 8(a), Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business (SDVOSB), Women-Owned Small Business (WOSB), or HUBZone. Also search “Total Small Business” opportunities when your size standard allows it. A set-aside designation is not a substitute for checking the solicitation’s exact eligibility language.

Read the **NAICS code and size standard** together. Your certification may qualify you for the set-aside while your revenue or employee count makes you ineligible under that particular code. Check whether the notice is a sole-source, competitive set-aside, or an unrestricted procurement with a small-business subcontracting goal.

## Three mistakes to avoid

1. **Searching only by a broad keyword.** “IT” or “construction” creates noise. Combine a specific service phrase, NAICS code, and geography, then save separate searches for adjacent services.
2. **Waiting for the final solicitation.** Early notices reveal the incumbent, draft requirements, and industry day dates. Responding to a Request for Information can help an agency shape a requirement that reflects your capabilities.
3. **Ignoring amendments and attachments.** SAM.gov notices change. Subscribe to updates and download every amendment, Q&A, and referenced document. A missed amendment can make an otherwise strong proposal nonresponsive.

## Build a repeatable pipeline

Set a weekly review cadence. For each candidate, record the agency, contracting office, set-aside type, NAICS, deadline, estimated value, incumbent, and one-sentence fit assessment. Then qualify quickly: can you meet the place of performance, security requirements, contract vehicle, and minimum experience?
