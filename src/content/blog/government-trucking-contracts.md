Government trucking contracts cover very different jobs. A notice that mentions a truck might require installation, hazardous materials handling, medical specimens, or equipment your carrier does not own. Start with your actual pickup and delivery capabilities, then check the solicitation's scope before deciding whether to bid.

## Search the work, not just “trucking”

Try several terms: freight transportation, pickup and delivery, flatbed, equipment transport, hauling, courier, and logistics. Search your service area and the states where a load could start or end. In [SAM.gov Contract Opportunities](https://sam.gov/opportunities), check the notice type and the **current response deadline** as well as the title. An award notice records a completed decision; a sources-sought notice asks for market research and is not an offer to award a contract.

NAICS codes help narrow the search. The [Census Bureau's NAICS descriptions](https://www.census.gov/naics/resources/archives/sect48-49.html) distinguish local general freight (484110), long-distance truckload (484121), local specialized trucking (484220), and long-distance specialized trucking (484230). Search more than one code if your business genuinely performs those services. A contract's NAICS code alone does not prove that its cargo, routes, or equipment fit your company.

## Check five things before you spend bid time

1. **Pickup and delivery points:** Can your fleet reach both ends on the required schedule? A contracting office in your state may buy work performed elsewhere.
2. **Cargo and equipment:** Check trailer type, loading duties, securement, liftgates, escorts, and any specialized certifications. A flatbed carrier should not assume it can perform installation or hazardous-waste handling.
3. **Eligibility:** Confirm the set-aside, size standard, registrations, and any required contract vehicle or past performance.
4. **Notice stage:** A solicitation invites a quote or proposal; a sources-sought notice collects capability information. A presolicitation may signal future work but can change.
5. **Deadline and documents:** Read amendments, the statement of work, pricing sheets, and Q&A on the original buyer page. Search aggregators can lag behind changed dates.

Reject an opportunity when one mandatory requirement does not fit. That saves more time than collecting a long list of superficially relevant bids.

## Build a useful weekly shortlist

For each candidate, record the official link, buyer, locations, cargo, required equipment, eligibility, due date, and one sentence on why it fits. Keep market-research notices separate from bids due now. If a deadline is already past or a mandatory meeting was missed, mark it closed even when a search tool still calls it active.
