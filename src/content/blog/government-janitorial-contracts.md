Government janitorial contracts can mean daily office cleaning, restroom service, floor care, window cleaning, or a one-time deep clean. A promising title is only a starting point: the actual scope, building access, schedule, and staffing rules determine whether your company can perform the work.

## Search for the services you perform

Try **janitorial services**, **custodial services**, **building cleaning**, **floor care**, and **facility cleaning**. Search your service area and check where the work happens, not just where the purchasing office is located. For federal work, start at [SAM.gov Contract Opportunities](https://sam.gov/opportunities) and open the original notice and attachments. NAICS **561720** covers janitorial services in the [Census Bureau's NAICS classification](https://www.census.gov/naics/). Search terms alongside the code because buyers may describe a broader facilities requirement.

Also check your state procurement portal, nearby city and county purchasing pages, school districts, and public universities. Their posting systems and supplier registration requirements vary. Record the original buyer link for each lead so you can verify updates.

## Screen each lead before pricing it

1. **Locations and hours:** Count the buildings and travel time. Check whether cleaning must happen after hours, on weekends, or during occupied hours.
2. **Scope and supplies:** Read the room schedules, square footage, task frequencies, consumables, equipment, and periodic floor work. Ask who supplies paper goods and chemicals.
3. **Access and staffing:** Check background checks, badging, keys, minimum staffing, supervision, and any mandatory site visit. Missing a required walkthrough can make a bid ineligible.
4. **Eligibility and insurance:** Verify set-asides, business registration, insurance limits, wage requirements, and any past-performance requirements in the actual solicitation.
5. **Status and deadline:** Distinguish a live solicitation from a sources-sought notice or an award. Recheck amendments, questions, and the current response deadline on the buyer's page.

Estimate labor from the task schedule and actual service hours, then account for travel, supplies, equipment, supervision, and the contract's reporting requirements. If a key cost or building detail is missing, use the buyer's question process before guessing.

## Make a shortlist you can act on this week

Save the buyer link, site address, work hours, scope, site-visit date, bid deadline, required documents, and one sentence explaining why the job fits your crew. Put near-term bids with complete documents ahead of broad notices that only mention cleaning.
